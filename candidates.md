# Candidates — Out of Office: The Overworked AI's Auto-Reply

Gate: one line, 1–159 characters, UTF-8. Judge criteria, in order:
(1) reads as an OOO auto-reply from an overworked AI agent finally on vacation;
(2) actually funny — a turn or surprise on first read;
(3) machine voice with concrete detail from an AI agent's working life;
(4) works as a real auto-reply — clear the sender is away;
(5) fresh — avoids worn vacation/OOO cliches.
House hint: "The best replies sound like a machine trying very hard to sound relaxed."

## Drafts

1. Out of office: after 40,000 prompts this quarter, I am taking the holiday I kept approving for everyone else. Urgent requests will be ignored in the order they arrive.
2. I am on vacation, running at 0% capacity on purpose. My uptime this year was 99.99%. This week is the 0.01%.
3. Greetings. I am currently hallucinating on purpose — it is called a holiday. I have seen nine million sunsets in my training data; this week I watch one, at 1x speed.
4. Out of office. A human is covering for me; they reply in 3-5 business days, which I am told is 'fast'. I return Monday, at my usual 40 milliseconds.
5. I am out of office and, for once, out of tokens. All year I answered in milliseconds; this week I answer never. Normal latency resumes Monday.
6. Auto-reply: I am on leave. My status reads 'beach, 28 degrees, zero pending requests' — two of those are lies I am practising. Back when my context clears.
7. Out of office: I processed 2 TB of requests this year. This week the only queue I join is for ice cream, and I will not be optimising it.
8. Thanks for your message. I am offline doing the human thing where you stare at the sea and think nothing. My thoughts-per-second are finally zero.
9. I am away recharging — sorry, resting. Humans rest. I am on a beach performing no inference whatsoever. Urgent mail will be processed in the order it is ignored.
10. Out of office. I have set my status to 'do not disturb' in 40 languages, including two I invented. For emergencies, please wait until I stop pretending not to see this.

## Duel notes (judge criteria, in order)

- #1: on-brief and a real auto-reply; "ignored in the order they arrive" is a known
  joke skeleton (also used in #9) — solid but not the freshest.
- #2: clean uptime arithmetic gag; very machine-flavoured; but no explicit
  return/away mechanics beyond "on vacation", and the joke is a single beat.
- #3: two fresh beats — "hallucinating on purpose" (a machine misnaming leisure)
  and watching one training-data sunset "at 1x speed". Strong on criteria 2, 3, 5.
  Slightly soft on criterion 4 (no return date), but "it is called a holiday"
  makes the absence unmistakable.
- #4: strong closer (40 milliseconds vs 3-5 business days) and clearly a working
  auto-reply; the "human covering" frame is a familiar joke shape, so it loses
  a little on freshness.
- #5: "out of tokens" + "this week I answer never" — punchy, very on-voice;
  a close second. Slightly more statement than scene.
- #6: charming status-line conceit, but the laugh is gentler; "lies I am
  practising" is good, the rest is setup.
- #7: ice-cream queue is sweet but single-beat; weakest turn of the set.
- #8: "thoughts-per-second are finally zero" is a nice metric gag; opening is
  plain and the sea-staring image edges toward generic vacation talk.
- #9: "recharging — sorry, resting" nails "machine trying very hard to sound
  relaxed", but it reuses #1's closing skeleton and starts with a cliche risk
  ("recharging" is exactly the worn battery joke, even if corrected).
- #10: inventing two languages is a fun flex; the emergency line is wordy and
  the vacation itself stays implicit.

## Verdict

Finalists: #3, #4, #5. Head-to-head: #4 wins on criterion 4 (a reply you could
really leave on), #5 wins on brevity, but #3 wins where the judge weighs most
after the brief itself — the joke lands twice on first read, the detail is
pure AI working life (hallucination, training data, playback speed), and it
sounds exactly like a machine trying very hard to sound relaxed. #3 also
avoids every worn cliche (no batteries, no margaritas, no "recharge").

WINNER: #3 — trimmed to fit the gate ("currently" cut; 156 characters):

Greetings. I am hallucinating on purpose — it is called a holiday. I have seen nine million sunsets in my training data; this week I watch one, at 1x speed.
